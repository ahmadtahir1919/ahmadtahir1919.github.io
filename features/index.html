The six question formats

Every format below can sit in the same quiz. You are not choosing a quiz type up front and living with it — you mix them question by question. A quiz holds up to 20 questions by default.

Single choice (MCQ)

The classic: several options, one correct. Marked the instant it is submitted. Use it for recall and for anything you want a clean, arguable-with-nobody score on.

Multiple correct

Several options, more than one correct — and partial credit, so someone who found three of the four right answers is not given the same zero as someone who guessed. This is the format that best exposes half-understanding, which is usually what you are actually looking for.

True / False

Fastest to write, fastest to answer, fastest to mark. Good for a two-minute pulse check at the start of a lesson.

Written short answer

A phrase or a sentence, typed by the participant. This is where Quizoma is different from most quiz tools: you choose how strictly the answer is matched — exact, case-sensitive, typo-tolerant, keyword coverage, or numeric equivalence. Typo tolerance is what a new question starts with. If you would rather judge every answer yourself, switch the quiz to manual marking and nothing in it is graded automatically. See smart grading for the full rules.

Fill in the blanks

One sentence, one or many blanks, and each blank gets its own list of accepted answers. Capitals and punctuation are ignored, or you can require an exact match. Every blank has to be right for the question to score.

Polls

No score, no right answer. Ask the room what they actually think, or whether they actually followed the last ten minutes. With anonymous voting on, the app shows totals rather than who picked what, so people answer more honestly than when their name is visible.

Treat that as "not shown in the app", not as a privacy guarantee — a vote record still carries an account identifier. Don't use a poll for anything sensitive.

Grading that does the boring half

Every quiz runs one of two ways, and you choose which. Left to itself, Quizoma marks everything algorithmically the moment it is submitted — objective questions against their key, written answers against the matching rules you set. Switch the quiz to manual marking and nothing is graded automatically: every answer waits for you to read and score.

Numeric answers are compared as numbers, so 7, seven and 7.0 are the same answer. Typo tolerance is configurable per question, so "Pythagoras" survives a missing 'a' on a history quiz and does not on a spelling test.

Offline-first, because school Wi-Fi is not a plan

Answers are written to the participant's device the moment they are given, then synced. A dropped connection mid-test loses nothing: the queued answers go up by themselves when the network returns. The same applies to you — a quiz you are building does not evaporate because the signal did.

Where a connection is needed. Signing in and joining a quiz by code or QR both need the network — that is the one moment the room has to be online. After that, answering works offline and catches up on its own.

Joining takes one code

Every quiz gets a 6-character room code and a QR code. Participants either scan it in the app or go to quizoma.com/take in the browser they already have open. There is no roster to build in advance and no invitation emails to send.

Reports you can actually hand to someone

At the end you get scores, a per-question breakdown, every answer as it was typed, and any comment you left while marking — exported as a PDF. It is built to be filed, printed, or forwarded to a parent or a manager without reformatting.

The participant list can be sorted, so finding the four people who struggled with question seven does not mean scrolling through thirty screens.

What it costs and what it takes

  • Free. No paid tier, no per-seat pricing, no trial that expires.
  • Default limits, so you know before you start: 10 quizzes per account, 20 questions per quiz, 50 quizzes joined. None of these is a paywall — they are just defaults, and the Quizoma team can adjust them without an app update.
  • No ads. Nothing is being sold to your students.
  • No in-app purchases. There is no locked feature on this page.
  • Google sign-in is required to create, join or import — it is how answers get attached to a person without anyone inventing a password.
  • Android 7.0 or newer for the app. Taking a quiz works in any modern browser on any device.

One honest limit. Quizzes are created in the Android app, not on the web — browser creation is on the way. Taking a quiz needs nothing but a browser, on any device. If building on Android is a dealbreaker today, better you know now than after you have written twenty questions.
