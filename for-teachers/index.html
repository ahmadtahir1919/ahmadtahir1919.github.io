Four things that decide whether a tool survives in a real classroom

Most quiz apps demo beautifully and then meet a Tuesday afternoon. These are the four places they usually break, and what Quizoma does about each.

1. It has to cost nothing

Quizoma is free. Not a trial, not a free tier with your class size capped at ten, not "free until the school gets billed in September". There is no paid plan to be upgraded to, because there isn't one. You do not have to ask anybody for money to use it on Monday.

2. It cannot show ads to children

There are no ads anywhere in the app or on this site. Your students are not an audience being sold to while they answer a chemistry question. There are no in-app purchases either, so nothing in a lesson turns into a prompt to buy something.

3. It has to work when the Wi-Fi doesn't

School networks fall over — usually at the exact moment thirty devices try to use them at once. Quizoma writes every answer to the participant's own device the instant it is given, then syncs. A dropped connection mid-test costs nothing: the answers queue up and go when the network returns. Nobody restarts, nobody loses their work, and you do not have to apologise to a class.

To be exact about it: joining needs a connection, because the code has to be looked up and everyone signs in. Once they are in, the network can drop and the quiz keeps going. Getting the room joined early is the one thing worth doing while the Wi-Fi is behaving.

4. It must not create marking

An auto-grader that marks a fair answer wrong is worse than no auto-grader, because now you have to check all of it. So Quizoma lets you decide how a written answer is judged: a close spelling, a number written as a word, or an explanation that covers the right ideas can all count. And if a quiz is one you would rather mark yourself, switch it to manual marking and nothing in it is graded automatically. See how smart grading works for the five matching rules and what each one is for.

Joining does not need a roster

You do not build a class list in advance, import a CSV, or collect email addresses. Quizoma generates a 6-character code and a QR. Put it on the board. Students with the app scan it; everyone else opens quizoma.com/take in the browser already on their phone. There is nothing for them to install and nothing for you to administer.

They sign in with a Google account so their answers are attached to a real person — which is what you need for a graded test — but there is no separate Quizoma password for anyone to forget in week three.

Ask the questions you actually wanted to ask

Multiple choice is easy to mark and easy to guess. The useful questions are usually the ones students have to write. Quizoma handles both in the same quiz:

Written short answer

A phrase or sentence, graded by a rule you choose — including keyword coverage, so "explain why" questions can be partly auto-marked instead of not at all.

Fill in the blanks

Each blank has its own accepted answers and its own mark. Two of three right scores two of three.

Multiple correct

Partial credit included. The format that shows you who half-understands, which is usually what you needed to know.

Polls

No score. "Did that make sense?" gets a franker answer when the app shows totals rather than names. Not a privacy guarantee — don't use it for anything sensitive.

Evidence you can hand to someone else

Every quiz produces a PDF report: scores, a per-question breakdown, every answer exactly as it was typed, and any comment you left while marking. That covers the parent meeting, the department file, and the moment somebody asks why a grade was what it was. The participant list sorts, so finding everyone who missed question seven is one tap rather than thirty screens.

Where else it fits

The design is classroom-first, but the same mechanics work anywhere people answer questions in a room: corporate training and compliance checks, study groups, tutoring, club quiz nights, and induction sessions. Polls in particular tend to get used far outside school.

Honest limits before you commit a lesson to it

Quizzes are created in the Android app, not in a browser — web creation is on the way. Your students can take a quiz on anything with a browser, whatever they happen to own. Everyone signs in with a Google account. If that does not work for your school, it is better to know now.

What we do with your data

Short version: the data exists to run the quiz and produce your report, and it is not sold. The full detail — what is stored, for how long, and how to remove it — is in the privacy policy, and any account can be deleted from the account deletion page.
